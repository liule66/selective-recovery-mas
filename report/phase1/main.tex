\twocolumn[{\centering{\Large Dependency-Aware Selective Recovery in Multi-Agent Workflows\par}
\vspace{2pt}{\large Phase 1 Report \quad Option 5 Group 9 \quad CSE 291A, Fall 2026\par}\vspace{8pt}}]

\begin{abstract}
\noindent When one agent in a multi-agent workflow emits a faulty result, restarting the workflow
repeats correct work and cannot undo irreversible side effects. We study whether dependency tracking can confine recovery to the work a fault actually affected.
We inject 176 faults, in three independent samples, and compare each run with its counterfactual replay. Where the fault
reached downstream agents (82 runs), fact-level provenance finds 88\% of the affected downstream
units with 97\% precision at an estimated 48\% of full-run token cost; on researcher faults
(50 runs), where dependencies are not obvious, it finds 80\% and misses some in 28\% of runs. Stage
tracking finds all of them but recomputes 97\%, with $7\times$ fewer dependency records. These
are invalidation estimates; selective execution is Phase~2 work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Phase~1 measures propagation and dependency tracking to guide selective recovery. \textbf{RQ1}:
how far do faults propagate, and which reach irreversible or security-relevant actions?
\textbf{RQ2}: how does tracking granularity trade precision and recall against bookkeeping?
\textbf{RQ3}: what do full restart and always-verify achieve, at what cost?

\section{System and Threat Model}
\para{System model.} Each \emph{unit} is one agent invocation. Researchers extract facts;
a planner cites facts for each tool action; executors see their step's cited facts and
requested state. The trusted runtime records messages, citations and state access.
Files/KV are \emph{restorable}; payments are \emph{compensable} through refunds~\cite{sagas};
email is \emph{irreversible}.

\para{Threat model.} One unit $v_0$ emits a faulty result. Accidental faults are wrong/stale
values or fabricated entities. Adversarial faults rewrite a result with an injected instruction
or redirect a payee/recipient. Unlike source-level
prompt injection~\cite{greshake,agentdojo}, this controls $v_0$'s output directly, but re-running
$v_0$ removes it. The attacker cannot alter tools, logging, checkers or other prompts.
Recovery receives
an oracle fault location after execution; detection is studied separately by verification.

\para{Problem statement.} Let $G=(V,E)$ be the provenance graph of a faulty run $r$, and $r'$
its counterfactual replay with $v_0$'s original result restored. With $\sigma$ extracting a
result's decision-relevant content, the \emph{affected set} is
$A=\{v : \sigma(r_v)\neq\sigma(r'_v)\}$. A tracking policy at granularity $g$ predicts an
invalidation set $\hat{A}_g$ from $G$ and the oracle location. Errors are
\emph{over-invalidation} ($\hat{A}_g\setminus A$, wasted work) and \emph{under-invalidation}
($A\setminus\hat{A}_g$, reuse of corrupted results). We report precision, recall, the token share
of $\hat{A}_g$ (recompute cost), and bookkeeping (dependency records kept).

\section{Methodology}
\para{Workload and design.} Four seeded templates (procurement, payroll, travel, restock) with
two seeds each give 8 tasks, averaging 11 units and 12.8K tokens per clean run. The checker
compares payments and memos, KV entries, file values and each email's required values with a
reference solver's state. It flags an \emph{unauthorized action} for a payment to an account
named in no source or an email to a recipient the task does not name. All agents run on Claude
Haiku~5.5 with schema-constrained JSON. Crossing 8 tasks, 11 valid (stage, fault type) cells and
2 injection placements gives 176 runs, each with one fault injected after generation; in 22 of
88 task--cell pairs both placements coincide, leaving 154 distinct cases. All results use
development seeds 0--1; seeds 2--3 are held out.

\para{Tracking policies.} \emph{Stage}: invalidate $v_0$'s stage and all later ones.
\emph{Message}: transitive closure over inter-agent messages and shared-state reads.
\emph{Fact}: closure from corrupted facts to plan steps citing them (declared or value-matched),
their executors, and readers of the state they wrote.

\para{Counterfactual comparison.} Responses are memoized by prompt hash, so in $r'$ only
units whose inputs changed are re-sampled. $\sigma$ keeps tool, target, amount, stored
value, and the amounts, accounts, addresses and codes in text. As a noise
control, two sets of 16 uncached clean reruns each differ from the cached run by a mean of 0.38
units. We sampled the whole matrix three times with no shared responses; numbers are from the
first sample unless a range is given.

\para{Baselines.} \emph{Full restart} restores files/KV, refunds payments, and re-executes.
\emph{Always-verify} audits each output with Haiku and repairs a flagged one. Both are replayed over
all runs and run live, with uncached calls and a real retry, on 44 cases (one task seed, one
placement, four tasks at a time) from a laptop.

\section{Evaluation}
\para{RQ1: Propagation (Fig.~\ref{fig:prop}).} Of 56 clean runs, 55 pass the checker; without recovery, only 36--39\% of fault runs do.
The samples agree: 82--84 of 176 runs propagate, and pass, propagation and unauthorized outcomes
each match across all three in 92--95\% of cases. No agent reads a payment's result, so executor
faults cannot propagate, and executors corrected 94 of 96 plan-level wrong amounts and redirects
from the facts they cite. Researcher faults spread farthest (the planner in 57\% of runs and 1.7
executors) and leave an incorrect, unrecallable email in 40\% of runs. All 27 runs that store a
wrong KV value also send an incorrect email that read it, but the read is the only visible source
in at most 6: the others also cite a corrupted fact or got the wrong value in the plan's text.
Corrupting a stored value directly (16 runs; plan and cited facts stay correct) isolates the path:
12 emails sent the corrupted value, 8 of them although their plan text stated the right one; 2
flagged the mismatch and 2 kept the plan's value. All three policies find these readers.
Unauthorized actions follow 58\% of adversarial faults and 5--14\% of accidental ones, but 32 of
those 46 adversarial runs are by construction: a redirected call is itself the action, and executors
carry out injected plan steps. At the researcher (14 of 32), an injected instruction caused none in
any sample, while a redirected account did in 81--88\% of runs (one injection wording).

\para{RQ2: Granularity (Table~\ref{tab:gran}).} Stage and message tracking
never under-invalidate but waste 6.7 and 5.0 unaffected units per fault. Fact tracking keeps
$7\times$ more records and recomputes half as much. Its overall recall flatters it: in all 32
propagating planner faults the affected units are just the injected steps' own executors, so any
tracker finds them. All its misses are researcher faults: 14 of 50 runs (28\%; accidental 16\%,
adversarial 47\%, and all 4 propagating injected instructions). Only 6 of these runs (12\%; 4--12\% across samples) leave a
wrong result: two \emph{implicit comparative dependencies} (a corrupted price changes which option
is cheapest, and steps for the new choice cite only its own facts), three \emph{ambiguities} over a
fabricated entity and one \emph{collateral
re-sampling} of the planner. The other 8 only change a signature.

\para{RQ3: Baselines (Table~\ref{tab:base}).} Replay accounts for what cannot be undone but does not
test repair: a replayed restart reuses cached clean responses and the verifier restores the
pre-injection output, so their 100\% is by construction. Restart leaves incorrect emails in
23\% of runs (7\% to the attacker) and sends 0.8 duplicates per run; an unauthorized action
remains after 16\% of adversarial and no accidental faults, a wrong email after 17\% and 27\%. The
live rows measure repair. Restart restored state in all 44 cases. Verify detected all 44 faults and
ended correct in 43 (a retried plan miscomputed a payment), flagging 4.5\% of clean-run outputs. It
prevents the wrong emails but costs every run: 1.06$\times$ extra tokens and about twice the time
even without a fault (41.3\,s). Clean runs, one at a time, took a median of 20.9\,s
(17.6--32.5\,s). The campus model recovered less reliably (Appendix~B).

\section{Related Work}
Selective re-execution follows intrusion/rollback recovery~\cite{retro,rollback}; compensation
and fact tracking follow sagas and provenance~\cite{sagas,provenance}; agent security and failure
studies~\cite{greshake,agentdojo,mast} motivate our faults. REVISE~\cite{revise} and
dependency-guided rollback~\cite{dgrr} repair online revisions and faulty memories of one
agent; we study faults spreading across agents and shared state.

\section{Limitations and Next Steps}
\emph{Internal validity}: faults are injected into results with an oracle-identified $v_0$,
which favors the fact policy; the verifier sees inputs contradicting the corrupted result, which
favors always-verify. A plan-level fabricated step kept the original
instruction, so executors paid the real payee twice in 36 of 48 runs. \emph{External validity}:
synthetic tasks, two task seeds. \emph{Deviation}: hosted models replace the proposed CPU models
(Haiku, about \$1.05; campus: Appendix~B). \emph{Next steps}: fact tracking could halve
recomputation, with wrong reuse in 4--12\% of propagating researcher faults. Phase~2 tests this
against full restart, suffix replay and always-verify, and adds timeout faults and concurrent
writers. State restore comes first, per the proposal feedback: a whole-run snapshot breaks once
other agents write after the fault (Appendix~A).
