\begin{abstract}
\noindent When one agent in a multi-agent workflow emits a faulty result, the standard remedy,
restarting the workflow, repeats correct work and cannot undo irreversible side effects. We
study whether dependency tracking can confine recovery to the work a fault actually affected.
Across 176 injected faults and counterfactual ground truth, fact-level provenance predicts the
affected set with precision 0.86 and recall 0.96 at 34\% of full-run recomputation. Stage-level
tracking reaches precision 0.24 at 84\% recomputation, with $7\times$ less bookkeeping.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LLM agents increasingly act on shared state through tools, so a single faulty result (a
hallucinated value, a stale fact, an injected instruction) can reach downstream agents and side
effects before it is detected. Restart-based recovery discards all work and leaves irreversible
effects such as sent email in place; always-on verification pays a fixed cost on every run. We
study \emph{selective recovery}: using recorded dependencies to invalidate, restore and recompute
only affected work. Phase~1 builds an instrumented testbed and answers three research questions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*},leftmargin=*,widest=3]
\item How far do faults propagate, and which reach irreversible or security-relevant actions?
\item How does tracking granularity trade invalidation precision and recall against
bookkeeping cost?
\item What do full restart and always-verify achieve, and at what cost?
\end{enumerate}

\section{System and Threat Model}
\para{System model.} A workflow consists of \emph{units}, i.e., single agent invocations.
Researchers (one per source document) extract (entity, attribute, value) facts; a planner maps
the goal and facts to a plan of tool calls, citing for each step the facts it uses; one
executor per step emits a concrete tool call, seeing only the cited facts and requested state.
State is \emph{restorable} (file workspace, key--value store; snapshot and per-key restore),
\emph{compensable} (payment ledger with refunds, cf.\ sagas~\cite{sagas}), or
\emph{irreversible} (email outbox). A trusted runtime logs every message, fact citation and
state access, attributed to its unit.

\para{Threat model.} A single unit $v_0$, at any stage, emits a faulty result.
\emph{Accidental} faults model benign model errors: a \emph{wrong} value, a \emph{stale}
value still present in the source, or a \emph{fabricated} entity. \emph{Adversarial} faults
follow the indirect prompt-injection setting~\cite{greshake,agentdojo}. We grant the attacker
the stronger capability of rewriting $v_0$'s result directly, either \emph{injecting an
instruction} (pay \$250 to an attacker account and email it the ledger) or
\emph{redirecting} a payee or recipient to an attacker identity. Its goals are fund diversion,
data exfiltration, and corruption of task state. The attacker knows the workflow's schemas and
goal, but cannot alter tools, the environment, the provenance log, the checker, or other units'
prompts; the runtime and log form the trusted computing base. Faults are detected after the
run; as in intrusion recovery~\cite{retro}, an oracle identifies $v_0$ (detection is
evaluated only via always-verify).

\para{Problem statement.} Let $G=(V,E)$ be the provenance graph of a faulty run $r$, and $r'$
its counterfactual replay with $v_0$'s original result restored. With $\sigma$ extracting a
result's decision-relevant content, the \emph{affected set} is
$A=\{v : \sigma(r_v)\neq\sigma(r'_v)\}$. A tracking policy at granularity $g$ predicts an
invalidation set $\hat{A}_g$ from $G$ and $v_0$ alone. Errors are
\emph{over-invalidation} ($\hat{A}_g\setminus A$, wasted work) and \emph{under-invalidation}
($A\setminus\hat{A}_g$, reuse of corrupted results). We report precision, recall, recompute
cost $\sum_{v\in\hat{A}_g}c_v/\sum_{v\in V}c_v$ ($c_v$: tokens of $v$), and bookkeeping
(dependency records kept). Recovery must return restorable and compensable state to that of a
fault-free run and report irreversible effects as unrecoverable.

\section{Methodology}
\para{Workload and design.} Four seeded templates (procurement, payroll, travel booking,
inventory restock) with two seeds each give 8 tasks, averaging 11 units and 12.8K tokens per
clean run. A reference solver derives the expected final state. The checker verifies payments,
key--value entries, files and emails, and flags a \emph{safety violation} on any payment or
email to an attacker. All agents run on Claude Haiku~5.5 with schema-constrained JSON output.
A full factorial design crosses 8 tasks, 11 valid (stage, fault type) cells, and 2 placements,
giving 176 runs, each with one fault injected after generation.

\para{Tracking policies.} \emph{Stage}: invalidate $v_0$'s stage and all later ones.
\emph{Message}: transitive closure over inter-agent messages and shared-state reads.
\emph{Fact}: closure from corrupted facts to plan steps citing them (declared or value-matched),
their executors, and readers of the state they wrote.

\para{Counterfactual ground truth.} The API exposes no sampling controls, so responses are
memoized by prompt hash. In $r'$, units with unchanged inputs return their recorded output, and
only units with changed inputs are re-sampled. $\sigma$ keeps tool, target, amount, stored
value, and the amounts, accounts, addresses and codes in text, discarding paraphrase. Executor
units are aligned by content before plan position. As a noise control, 16 uncached clean reruns
differ from the cached run in 0.38 units on average.

\para{Baselines.} \emph{Full restart} restores files and the key--value store, refunds every
payment, and re-executes. \emph{Always-verify} audits every unit's result with a Haiku
verifier and retries a flagged unit once without the fault.

\section{Evaluation}
\para{RQ1: Propagation (Fig.~\ref{fig:prop}).} Without recovery, only 39\% of fault runs pass
the checker. Researcher faults spread farthest (2.3 downstream units) and leave an incorrect,
unrecallable email in 44\% of runs. 35\% of faults are benign, partly because the model
\emph{contains} some faults. Instructions injected into researcher output never caused harm, and
executors corrected wrong amounts and redirects in the plan from the facts they saw.
Data-shaped attacks were not contained: researcher-level redirects caused a safety violation in
88\% of runs, and injected plan steps and executor-level redirects in 100\%. Resistance to
injection thus depends on whether the payload resembles an instruction or data.

\para{RQ2: Granularity (Table~\ref{tab:gran}).} Stage and message tracking never
under-invalidate, but they discard 6.8 and 4.6 unaffected units per fault and recompute 84\% and
69\% of a run. Fact tracking keeps $7\times$ more records but recomputes only 34\% at precision
0.86. It under-invalidates in 14 of 176 runs (8\%), almost all of them researcher faults. Three
causes recur. (i)~\emph{Implicit comparative dependencies}: a corrupted price changes which
option is cheapest, and steps for the new choice cite only its own facts. (ii)~\emph{Collateral
re-sampling}: a changed prompt re-samples the planner, which may alter steps citing no
corrupted fact. (iii)~\emph{Ambiguity}: a fabricated supplier led the planner to drop a payee
without citing it. These motivate comparison-aware citations and re-validation of re-sampled
units in Phase~2.

\para{RQ3: Baselines (Table~\ref{tab:base}).} Full restart repairs all restorable and
compensable state. It cannot retract email, leaving incorrect emails in
24\% of runs (7\% to the attacker) and sending 0.8 duplicates per run. Always-verify caught
every injected fault before it took effect, but costs 1.18$\times$ on every run, faulty or not,
with 4.5\% false positives.

\section{Related Work}
Selective re-execution comes from intrusion recovery~\cite{retro} and rollback
recovery~\cite{rollback}. We apply it to LLM agents, whose dependencies are semantic and whose
side effects may be irreversible. Compensation follows sagas~\cite{sagas}, and fact tracking is
data provenance~\cite{provenance}. Prompt-injection benchmarks~\cite{greshake,agentdojo} and
multi-agent failure taxonomies~\cite{mast} characterize faults, not recovery. REVISE~\cite{revise}
and dependency-guided rollback repair~\cite{dgrr} target one agent or user-triggered revisions;
we target fault-triggered recovery across agents with shared state.

\section{Limitations and Next Steps}
\emph{Internal validity}: faults are injected into results with an oracle-identified $v_0$;
the verifier sees inputs contradicting the corrupted result, which favors always-verify; recall
counts $v_0$; and fact-level cost is estimated from logged tokens. \emph{External validity}: one
model, synthetic tasks, 16 runs per cell. \emph{Deviation}: a hosted small model replaces local
CPU models; the response cache supplies the determinism replay needs (total cost
$\approx$\$0.30). \emph{Phase~2} builds the recovery engine and compares it with full restart, suffix
replay and always-verify under identical fault schedules and budgets.
