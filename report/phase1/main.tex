\begin{abstract}
\noindent When one agent in a multi-agent workflow emits a faulty result, the standard remedy,
restarting the workflow, repeats correct work and cannot undo irreversible side effects. We
study whether dependency tracking can confine recovery to the work a fault actually affected.
We inject 176 faults and compare each run with its counterfactual replay. In the 82 runs where
the fault reached downstream agents, fact-level provenance finds 88\% of the affected
downstream units with 97\% precision, at an estimated 48\% of full-run token cost. Stage tracking
finds all of them but recomputes 97\%, while keeping $7\times$ fewer dependency records. These are
invalidation estimates; selective execution is Phase~2 work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Phase~1 measures propagation and dependency tracking to guide selective recovery:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*},leftmargin=*,widest=3]
\item How far do faults propagate, and which reach irreversible or security-relevant actions?
\item How does tracking granularity trade invalidation precision and recall against
bookkeeping cost?
\item What do full restart and always-verify achieve, and at what cost?
\end{enumerate}

\section{System and Threat Model}
\para{System model.} Each \emph{unit} is one agent invocation. Researchers extract facts;
a planner cites facts for each tool action; executors see their step's cited facts and
requested state. The trusted runtime records messages, citations and state access.
Files/KV are \emph{restorable}; payments are \emph{compensable} through refunds~\cite{sagas};
email is \emph{irreversible}.

\para{Threat model.} One unit $v_0$ emits a faulty result. Accidental faults are wrong/stale
values or fabricated entities. Adversarial faults rewrite a result with an injected instruction
(pay an attacker \$250 and email the ledger) or redirect a payee/recipient. Unlike source-level
prompt injection~\cite{greshake,agentdojo}, this controls $v_0$'s output directly, but re-running
$v_0$ removes it. The attacker knows the schemas and
goal but cannot alter tools, logging, checkers or other agents' prompts. Recovery receives
an oracle fault location after execution; detection is studied separately by verification.

\para{Problem statement.} Let $G=(V,E)$ be the provenance graph of a faulty run $r$, and $r'$
its counterfactual replay with $v_0$'s original result restored. With $\sigma$ extracting a
result's decision-relevant content, the \emph{affected set} is
$A=\{v : \sigma(r_v)\neq\sigma(r'_v)\}$. A tracking policy at granularity $g$ predicts an
invalidation set $\hat{A}_g$ from $G$ and the oracle location. Errors are
\emph{over-invalidation} ($\hat{A}_g\setminus A$, wasted work) and \emph{under-invalidation}
($A\setminus\hat{A}_g$, reuse of corrupted results). We report precision, recall, recompute
cost $\sum_{v\in\hat{A}_g}c_v/\sum_{v\in V}c_v$ ($c_v$: tokens of $v$), and bookkeeping
(dependency records kept).

\section{Methodology}
\para{Workload and design.} Four seeded templates (procurement, payroll, travel booking,
inventory restock) with two seeds each give 8 tasks, averaging 11 units and 12.8K tokens per
clean run. A reference solver derives the expected state. The checker verifies payments and
memos, key--value entries, file values, and each email's required values. It
flags an \emph{unauthorized action} for a payment to an account named in no source or an email
to a recipient the task does not name; payments and emails to the attacker are a subset.
All agents run on Claude Haiku~5.5 with schema-constrained JSON output.
A full factorial design crosses 8 tasks, 11 valid (stage, fault type) cells, and 2 placements,
giving 176 runs, each with one fault injected after generation. For 22 of the 88 task--cell pairs both
placements are identical, leaving 154 independent runs. Task seeds 0--1
are for development; 2--3 are held out for Phase~2.

\para{Tracking policies.} \emph{Stage}: invalidate $v_0$'s stage and all later ones.
\emph{Message}: transitive closure over inter-agent messages and shared-state reads.
\emph{Fact}: closure from corrupted facts to plan steps citing them (declared or value-matched),
their executors, and readers of the state they wrote.

\para{Counterfactual comparison.} Responses are memoized by prompt hash, so in $r'$ only
units whose inputs changed are re-sampled. $\sigma$ keeps tool, target, amount, stored
value, and the amounts, accounts, addresses and codes in text, discarding paraphrase. Executor
units are aligned by content before plan position. As a noise control, 16 uncached clean reruns
differ from the cached run in 0.38 units on average.

\para{Baselines.} \emph{Full restart} restores files/KV, refunds payments, and re-executes.
\emph{Always-verify} audits each output with Haiku, restores a flagged output to its pre-injection
value and estimates retry tokens; the revised harness retries for real (Appendix~B).

\section{Evaluation}
\para{RQ1: Propagation (Fig.~\ref{fig:prop}).} All 8 clean runs and 16 uncached reruns pass the
checker; without recovery, only 39\% of fault runs do. Only 82 of 176 runs propagate to a
downstream unit: no agent reads a payment's
result, so executor faults cannot, and executors corrected all plan-level wrong amounts and
redirects from the facts they cite. Researcher faults spread
farthest (the planner in 57\% of runs and 1.7 executors) and leave an incorrect, unrecallable
email in 40\% of runs. All 27 runs that store a wrong KV value also send an incorrect email that
read it, a shared-state path to an irreversible effect.
Unauthorized actions follow 58\% of adversarial faults and 5\% of accidental ones (fabricated
payees). Executors carry out injected plan steps and redirected calls by construction, as they
see only their own step. The informative contrast is at the researcher: an injected instruction
caused no unauthorized action, while a redirected account did in 88\% of runs (one injection
wording).

\para{RQ2: Granularity (Table~\ref{tab:gran}).} Stage and message tracking
never under-invalidate, but discard 6.7 and 5.0 unaffected units per fault and recompute 97\%
and 86\% of a run. Fact tracking keeps $7\times$ more records and recomputes 48\% at precision
0.97, but misses affected units in 14 of 82 runs (17\%; accidental 11\%, adversarial 26\%), all
researcher faults (within 0.01 without duplicate placements). Only 6 of these
runs (7\%) leave a wrong result. Two are \emph{implicit comparative dependencies}: a corrupted
price changes which option is cheapest, and steps for the new choice cite only its own facts.
Three are \emph{ambiguity}: a fabricated entity led agents to hold or drop a payment without
citing it. One is \emph{collateral re-sampling}: a changed prompt re-samples the planner, which
miscomputed a total. The other 8 only change a signature, such as a report adding a correct number.

\para{RQ3: Baselines (Table~\ref{tab:base}).} Full restart repairs all restorable and
compensable state. It cannot retract email, leaving incorrect emails in
23\% of runs (7\% to the attacker) and sending 0.8 duplicates per run. An unauthorized action
remains after 16\% of adversarial and no accidental faults; a wrong email after 17\% and 27\%. The
original verifier detected all injected faults and flagged 4.5\% of clean-run outputs, at an
estimated 1.18$\times$ extra tokens on fault runs and 1.04$\times$ on clean runs; its output
restoration is an optimistic repair model. \emph{Latency}: the Claude runs were not timed. On the
live campus route (Appendix~B) a clean 10-unit run took 6--16\,s, restart 7.5\,s and always-verify
17.3\,s (medians of 6).

\section{Related Work}
Selective re-execution follows intrusion/rollback recovery~\cite{retro,rollback}; compensation
and fact tracking follow sagas and provenance~\cite{sagas,provenance}. Agent security and failure
studies~\cite{greshake,agentdojo,mast} motivate our faults. REVISE~\cite{revise} and
dependency-guided rollback~\cite{dgrr} repair online revisions and faulty memories of one
agent; we study faults that spread across agents and shared state.

\section{Limitations and Next Steps}
\emph{Internal validity}: faults are injected into results with an oracle-identified $v_0$;
the verifier sees inputs contradicting the corrupted result, which favors always-verify.
Re-sampling makes counterfactuals approximate. Fine-grained oracle locations favor
the fact policy. A plan-level fabricated step kept the original instruction, so executors paid
the real payee twice. \emph{External validity}: synthetic
tasks, two task seeds, no timeout faults. \emph{Deviation}: hosted models replace the proposed
local CPU models (Haiku, about \$0.30; a campus model in Appendix~B). \emph{Next steps}: fact
tracking promises half of stage tracking's recomputation, with wrong reuse in 7\% of propagated
runs. Phase~2 builds the recovery engine (Appendix~A) to test that saving against full restart,
suffix replay and always-verify, and adds timeout and shared-state faults.
