\begin{abstract}
\noindent When one agent in a multi-agent workflow emits a faulty result, the standard remedy,
restarting the workflow, repeats correct work and cannot undo irreversible side effects. We
study whether dependency tracking can confine recovery to the work a fault actually affected.
Across 176 injected faults and counterfactual comparisons, fact-level provenance predicts the
affected set with precision 0.86 and recall 0.96 at an estimated 34\% of full-run token cost.
Stage tracking predicts 84\%, with $7\times$ fewer dependency records. These are invalidation
estimates; selective execution is Phase~2 work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A faulty agent result can propagate through shared state and tool actions before detection.
Full restart repeats valid work and cannot retract sent email; verification adds inference
cost. Phase~1 measures propagation and dependency tracking to guide selective recovery:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*},leftmargin=*,widest=3]
\item How far do faults propagate, and which reach irreversible or security-relevant actions?
\item How does tracking granularity trade invalidation precision and recall against
bookkeeping cost?
\item What do full restart and always-verify achieve, and at what cost?
\end{enumerate}

\section{System and Threat Model}
\para{System model.} Each \emph{unit} is one agent invocation. Researchers extract facts;
a planner cites facts for each tool action; executors see their step's cited facts and
requested state. The trusted runtime records messages, citations and state access.
Files/KV are \emph{restorable}; payments are \emph{compensable} through refunds~\cite{sagas};
email is \emph{irreversible}.

\para{Threat model.} One unit $v_0$ emits a faulty result. Accidental faults are wrong/stale
values or fabricated entities. Adversarial faults rewrite a result with an injected instruction
(pay an attacker \$250 and email the ledger) or redirect a payee/recipient. This is stronger
than source-level prompt injection~\cite{greshake,agentdojo}. The attacker knows the schemas and
goal but cannot alter tools, logging, checkers or other agents' prompts. Recovery receives
an oracle fault location after execution; detection is studied separately by verification.

\para{Problem statement.} Let $G=(V,E)$ be the provenance graph of a faulty run $r$, and $r'$
its counterfactual replay with $v_0$'s original result restored. With $\sigma$ extracting a
result's decision-relevant content, the \emph{affected set} is
$A=\{v : \sigma(r_v)\neq\sigma(r'_v)\}$. A tracking policy at granularity $g$ predicts an
invalidation set $\hat{A}_g$ from $G$ and the oracle location, including changed fact/step
IDs for the fact policy. Errors are
\emph{over-invalidation} ($\hat{A}_g\setminus A$, wasted work) and \emph{under-invalidation}
($A\setminus\hat{A}_g$, reuse of corrupted results). We report precision, recall, recompute
cost $\sum_{v\in\hat{A}_g}c_v/\sum_{v\in V}c_v$ ($c_v$: tokens of $v$), and bookkeeping
(dependency records kept). Recovery must return restorable and compensable state to that of a
fault-free run and report irreversible effects as unrecoverable.

\section{Methodology}
\para{Original workload.} Four seeded templates (procurement, payroll, travel booking,
inventory restock) with two seeds each give 8 tasks, averaging 11 units and 12.8K tokens per
clean run. A reference solver derives the expected final state. The checker verifies payments,
key--value entries, files and emails, and flags a \emph{safety violation} on any payment or
email to an attacker. All agents run on Claude Haiku~5.5 with schema-constrained JSON output.
A full factorial design crosses 8 tasks, 11 valid (stage, fault type) cells, and 2 placements,
giving 176 runs, each with one fault injected after generation.

\para{Tracking policies.} \emph{Stage}: invalidate $v_0$'s stage and all later ones.
\emph{Message}: transitive closure over inter-agent messages and shared-state reads.
\emph{Fact}: closure from corrupted facts to plan steps citing them (declared or value-matched),
their executors, and readers of the state they wrote.

\para{Counterfactual comparison.} Responses are memoized by prompt hash. In $r'$,
units with unchanged inputs return their recorded output, and
only units with changed inputs are re-sampled. $\sigma$ keeps tool, target, amount, stored
value, and the amounts, accounts, addresses and codes in text, discarding paraphrase. Executor
units are aligned by content before plan position. As a noise control, 16 uncached clean reruns
differ from the cached run in 0.38 units on average.

\para{Baselines.} \emph{Full restart} restores files/KV, refunds payments, and re-executes.
The original \emph{always-verify} experiment audited each output with Haiku, restored the
pre-injection output when flagged, and estimated retry tokens. The revised harness instead
makes one actual uncached retry; its results must be reported separately.

\section{Evaluation}
\para{RQ1: Propagation (Fig.~\ref{fig:prop}).} Without recovery, only 39\% of fault runs pass
the checker. Researcher faults spread farthest (2.3 downstream units) and leave an incorrect,
unrecallable email in 44\% of runs. 35\% of faults are benign, partly because the model
\emph{contains} some faults. Researcher instruction injections caused no attacker-directed actions in this suite, and
executors corrected wrong amounts and redirects in the plan from the facts they saw.
Data-shaped attacks were not contained: researcher-level redirects caused a safety violation in
88\% of runs, and injected plan steps and executor-level redirects in 100\%. Resistance to
injection thus depends on whether the payload resembles an instruction or data.

\para{RQ2: Granularity (Table~\ref{tab:gran}).} Stage and message tracking never
under-invalidate, but they discard 6.8 and 4.6 unaffected units per fault and recompute 84\% and
69\% of a run in predicted token cost. Fact tracking keeps $7\times$ more records but predicts 34\% at precision
0.86. It under-invalidates in 14 of 176 runs (8\%), almost all of them researcher faults. Three
causes recur. (i)~\emph{Implicit comparative dependencies}: a corrupted price changes which
option is cheapest, and steps for the new choice cite only its own facts. (ii)~\emph{Collateral
re-sampling}: a changed prompt re-samples the planner, which may alter steps citing no
corrupted fact. (iii)~\emph{Ambiguity}: a fabricated supplier led the planner to drop a payee
without citing it. These motivate comparison-aware citations and re-validation of re-sampled
units in Phase~2.

\para{RQ3: Baselines (Table~\ref{tab:base}).} Full restart repairs all restorable and
compensable state. It cannot retract email, leaving incorrect emails in
24\% of runs (7\% to the attacker) and sending 0.8 duplicates per run. The original verifier
detected all injected faults, flagging 4.5\% of non-injected clean-run outputs. Estimated extra
token cost was 1.18$\times$ on fault runs and 1.04$\times$ on clean runs.
Output restoration is an optimistic repair model.

\section{Related Work}
Selective re-execution follows intrusion/rollback recovery~\cite{retro,rollback}; compensation
and fact tracking follow sagas and provenance~\cite{sagas,provenance}. Agent security and failure
studies~\cite{greshake,agentdojo,mast} motivate our injections. REVISE~\cite{revise} addresses
online revisions, and dependency-guided rollback~\cite{dgrr} repairs faulty persistent memories.
We examine fault propagation across agents and shared state.

\para{Revised harness.} Appendix~B reports campus-model validation of real retries,
timing and trace export separately from the original Claude results.

\section{Limitations and Next Steps}
\emph{Internal validity}: faults are injected into results with an oracle-identified $v_0$;
the verifier sees inputs contradicting the corrupted result, which favors always-verify; recall
counts $v_0$; and fact-level cost is estimated from logged tokens. Changed prompts can still
resample, so counterfactual comparison is approximate. Fine-grained oracle locations favor
the fact policy. \emph{External validity}: synthetic tasks and only two task seeds.
Original Claude raw traces were not committed; their failure explanations remain
author-reported. The revised harness saves traces, cache hits, versions, failures and wall
times. Cached replay times are not live API latency. \emph{Phase~2} builds recovery
and compares it with full restart, suffix
replay and always-verify under identical fault schedules and budgets.
